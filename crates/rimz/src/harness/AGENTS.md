# Agent harness

Local contract for `crates/rimz/src/harness/` — the spawn, drive, and reclaim machinery for agent sessions. Extends [crates/rimz/AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md).

Topic detail lives in [fleet.md](../../../../docs/internals/harness/fleet.md) (spawn, address, resume, reclaim), [scripting.md](../../../../docs/internals/harness/scripting.md) (supervised runs), [loops.md](../../../../docs/internals/harness/loops.md) (loop scheduling, signals and wakes, and the assist log), and [budget.md](../../../../docs/internals/harness/budget.md) (dollar caps and the park), with the shared delivery substrate in [messaging.md](../../../../docs/internals/harness/messaging.md).

## Boundaries

- The harness owns layout specs, effective launch resolution, profile prompt-file validation, launch finalization, provider-process compilation and argv, launch reminder assembly (`launch_reminders.rs` owns the one `<system_reminder>` tag, its paragraph order, and the model line; the team and catalog producers return bodies and never wrap), supervised run transitions over `store::run` records, the durable blocking-wait policy and run-wake socket (`run_wake.rs`), rebirth inspection and materialization, cohort and lane qualification/resume planning, relaunch posture resolution (`resume::resolve_posture`, shared by restart, fork, and every resume path), lane restore materialization, loop scheduling, hidden runner domain, auto-continue policy, and idle-compaction policy.
- `resume::resolve_posture` returns the planner's `ResumeLaunchPosture` beside its degradation reason. `plan` owns both resume-argv DTOs and never imports `resume`; the recovery plan and its entries are `harness`-private, reached from outside `resume` only by `rebirth`.
- Harness delivery rides `message`; it never reimplements queues, gates, retries, or transcript audit.
- `scratch.rs` owns the shared team memory-file scan and advisory `blackboard.md` stage parser; launch reminders and cohort reports use the same rooted matching, deduplication, and probe-failure semantics.
- `schedule/signal.rs` owns the runtime signal vocabulary: `Signal`, `SignalSelector`, `WatchVerdict` and the `WatchOutcome` carrying it, the agent and team lifecycle mappings, and `fire_signal`. `WatchVerdict::label` is the one place a watched command's outcome is put into words, and every renderer of one (`compose_wake`, `rimz loop logs`, `rimz loop show`) calls it rather than composing its own; `wake_output_path` is likewise the one derivation of a wake's room tmp output file for the watcher and the elder alike. `WatchOutcome::measured` keeps the durable tail, measures `WatchOutcome.summary`, and records the agent-visible path through `sandbox::TmpView`; wake messages show that path, size, and line count without the tail. The persisted half sits below it in `store::event` (the `SignalName` grammar, the `agent`, `wake`, `team`, `ci`, and `pr` families reserved from user emitters, `SignalSource`, and `SignalEventPayload`), and `From<&Signal> for SignalEventPayload` is the one conversion into it: `Store::append_signal` takes that payload, so no store module reaches up for a harness signal type. A subscription resolves against the signal's whole family: an exact name delivers, another member of the family skips (recorded, no delivery), and a different family or a failed `--match` is ignored. A signal subscription stays armed across siblings; only a delivering fire consumes a one-shot. Firing is in-process, with no queue and no replay: whichever process observes the event fires the tasks armed for it right there, and a signal nobody subscribes to is gone. A detached watcher is single-flight through its `RuntimePaths` lock, and losing that lock is an outcome the elder resolves, never something the watcher retries. `run_watcher` is that whole watcher lifetime in one function (catalog gate, lock, check, signal append, fire), and it holds the lock through the fire so the elder cannot call the row lost mid-flight; `schedule/arm.rs` spawns the detached child and the CLI hands over the store and workspace. Watched commands check in once after 30m by default without being killed, then report their exit; watcher fires serialize under the lock and cancellation stops the whole process group.
- `run.rs` is policy over the `store::run` record, which owns the schema, the codec, and the terminal wake sender. Every status transition is a locked read-modify-write here, and `create`, `load`, and `list` stay as wrappers over the store codec so callers work from `StatePaths` and leave the runs directory to them. `run_wake.rs` is the receiving half of that wake: it binds the per-run socket, validates each frame by `(workspace_id, run_id)`, and treats the durable record as truth when a datagram is lost.
- `schedule/arm.rs` owns the shared delivery builder, caller-scoped signal guards, live-subscription dedupe, and session retirement; its armed receipt retains the compiled `LoadedTask`. `schedule/arming.rs` owns effective arming and source defaults through `ArmState::resolve`; catalog-row `LoadedTask::enable` owns enabling and strike clearing. The catalog-private `clear_overlays` coordinates replacement/removal cleanup; instance insertion keeps separate arming and strike cleanup calls with distinct error paths. `schedule/team.rs` materializes declared role bindings at root-member registration with the adopted session identity. Instance rows live per workspace; session end, loss, and explicit stop retire pinned rows and their overlays. Self timer/command wakes steer with `Type: WAKE`; signal deliveries park at `done` with `Type: SIGNAL`.
- `schedule/runner.rs` owns the stop ladder as `stop_task`: run-lock probe, durable cancellation, grace wait, SIGTERM, grace wait, the `canceled` history row, and the manual-retry error. The CLI supplies the cancellation as a closure and prints the outcome.
- Terminal loop history rows are appended in the harness only, through the one `run_log` transition; the CLI renders the transition it gets back and never writes a row. `run_log::LoopRunResult::spawn_exit_code` maps terminal spawn results to `store::run::RunStatus` and delegates exit codes to it; renderers keep no separate numeric table.
- The agent-address grammar, its renderer, pane binding, and the launch-instance grouping with its lineage projections live in the top-level `address` module at the message layer; `message`, `harness`, the CLI, and one verdicted `agents` renderer site reach it, and `address` reaches only `agents`, `store`, and `ids`. Handle rendering and address resolution read the addressable peer set; raw root rows are for accounting scans. Minting a handle and deciding whether a name is legal belong to `agents::petname`, which the harness calls.
- CLI handlers keep argument parsing, presentation, and cross-command orchestration. Harness modules own pure domain rules, durable records, helper argv shape, and side-effect boundaries.
- Elder-fired helpers in `schedule/fire.rs`, `auto_continue.rs`, `auto_redeem.rs`, and `idle_compact.rs` spawn hidden CLI subprocesses with fresh null stdio.
- `auto_continue.rs`, `auto_redeem.rs`, `budget.rs`, `idle_compact.rs`, `orphan_sweep.rs`, `run_timeout.rs`, and `schedule/pending.rs` are in the sidebar import graph. Keep their evaluation side read-only and delegate durable mutations or provider transport to detached helpers; runtime-cache writes through `disk::atomic::write_temp_then_rename_cache` are the allowed durability path. Pending wakes read the lenient catalog without locks, migration, or writes.
- Assist records append from detached helper CLI handlers and the loop runner; sidebar-graph modules only pass the evidence those writers need.
